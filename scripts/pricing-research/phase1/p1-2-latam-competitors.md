### § 1. Local Productivity / Task / Budgeting Apps in LatAm

#### 1.1 Overview of Local App Ecosystem

The LatAm productivity and personal finance app landscape is notably fragmented compared to North America or Europe. The market is dominated by global apps (Todoist, Notion, Google Tasks, Microsoft To-Do) with local pricing adaptation rather than true local competitors — with a few important exceptions in budgeting. There is a significant gap in neurodivergent-specific tooling: **no identified LatAm-origin app addresses neurodivergent productivity explicitly** as of early 2026.

#### 1.4 Key Insight: The Local App Gap

The most significant finding is the **absence of any LatAm-origin app** combining task management + calendar + budgeting with explicit neurodivergent design. Local apps are almost exclusively single-function (budget only, or habit only). The closest analogues (Mobills, Organizze) focus on budgeting only and lack task/calendar/cognitive support features. This represents a genuine white space.

### § 2. How Global Apps Price FOR LatAm (PPP Adjustments)

#### 2.1 The PPP Pricing Landscape — Conceptual Framework

Global SaaS companies take three general approaches to LatAm pricing:
1. **No adjustment** (same USD price globally) — common for B2B SaaS, some consumer apps
2. **Partial PPP adjustment** (~40–60% of US price) — Spotify, Netflix, some Adobe products
3. **Full localized pricing** (~20–35% of US price) — rare; mostly gaming and streaming

The **purchasing power parity (PPP) ratio** between LatAm countries and the US, corrected against World Bank ICP 2021 data extrapolated through 2023, and IMF WEO data: [World Bank PA.NUS.PPPC.RF](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/PA.NUS.PPPC.RF) | [IMF WEO Datamapper](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/PPPEX@WEO)

| Country | Phase 1 Estimated Ratio | ~~Corrected~~ Verified Ratio (2026) | Notes |
|---------|------------------------|--------------------------------------|-------|
| **Peru** | ~~0.28~~ | **~0.39–0.41** | Phase 1 value was stale/incorrect basket; updated per World Bank ICP 2021 extrapolated |
| **Mexico** | ~~0.38~~ | **~0.46–0.49** | Revised upward per IMF WEO + World Bank data |
| **Colombia** | ~~0.31~~ | **~0.37–0.42** | Post-pandemic exchange rate movements account for the shift |
| **Brazil** | ~0.47 | **~0.43–0.52** (midpoint ~0.47) | Wide range due to BRL/USD volatility; original midpoint confirmed plausible |
| **Chile** | ~0.55 | **~0.53–0.60** | Original value confirmed plausible; most expensive LatAm economy |

> **Industry evidence on PPP pricing impact:** "Most major SaaS companies like Spotify, Slack, Netflix, and many more have already implemented PPP pricing for their solutions, and makers are reporting at least a 20% increase in revenue." "A $9.99 monthly subscription might feel manageable in the U.S. but becomes significantly pricier in regions where local currencies have weakened." — [Mirava.io](https://www.mirava.io/blog/why-global-app-revenue-drops-pricing-solutions)

#### 2.4 Notion Pricing in LatAm

As of early 2026, Notion does **not** offer localized PPP pricing in LatAm. All prices are billed in USD: [notion.com/pricing](https://www.notion.com/pricing)

| Plan | Price (USD/month) | Availability in LatAm | Notes |
|------|------------------|-----------------------|-------|
| Free | $0 | Yes | Feature-limited |
| Plus | **$10/user/mo (annual); $12/user/mo (monthly)** | Yes — USD only | ~~Phase 1 stated $8–10/mo annual — floor is $10, not $8~~ |
| Business | $18/user/mo | Yes — USD only | — |

**Practical effect:** A Colombian Notion Plus user pays ~COP 40,500/month at 2024 exchange rates — equivalent to approximately **2.3x** what would be considered PPP-fair pricing. This creates significant affordability friction and drives adoption of the free tier or account-sharing workarounds. Community forums (Reddit r/Colombia, r/Notion) document users sharing team accounts or using edu discounts to circumvent full pricing. Notion's conversion rate in LatAm is estimated to be significantly below its North America/Europe benchmark, though Notion does not publish regional conversion data.

#### 2.6 Apple App Store Pricing Tiers in LatAm

Apple restructured its App Store pricing tiers globally in 2023, introducing 900 price points and enabling more granular localized pricing. As of 2024, the pricing relationships between Apple's LatAm storefronts and the US are approximately:

| Country | App Store Currency | Tier 1 (lowest paid) | Tier 10 (~$2 equiv.) | Tier 20 (~$5 equiv.) | FX vs. USD | PPP adjustment built in? |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|---------------------|---------------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| USA | USD | $0.99 | $1.99 | $4.99 | 1.00 | Baseline |
| Mexico | MXN | MXN 17 (~$1.00) | MXN 35 (~$2.06) | MXN 89 (~$5.24) | ~17:1 | Minimal — roughly tracks FX |
| Colombia | COP | COP 3,900 (~$0.96) | COP 7,900 (~$1.95) | COP 19,900 (~$4.91) | ~4,060:1 | Minimal |
| Brazil | BRL | BRL 5.90 (~$1.17) | BRL 11.90 (~$2.36) | BRL 29.90 (~$5.93) | ~5.05:1 | **Slightly above USD** due to BRL volatility |
| Peru | PEN | PEN 3.90 (~$1.04) | PEN 7.90 (~$2.11) | PEN 19.90 (~$5.32) | ~3.75:1 | Minimal |
| Chile | CLP | CLP 990 (~$1.10) | CLP 1,990 (~$2.21) | CLP 4,990 (~$5.54) | ~900:1 | Minimal |

*Source: Apple App Store Connect Pricing documentation; developer community reports; AppFollow analysis 2024*

**Critical insight:** Apple's LatAm pricing tiers track **exchange rates but NOT purchasing power**. A $4.99 app in Peru costs the local equivalent of 3+ hours of minimum wage labor vs. ~30 minutes in the US. Apple does not apply true PPP adjustment in its standard tier structure. Developers must manually choose lower price tiers to achieve PPP-appropriate pricing.

[GSMA LatAm 2024](https://www.gsma.com/about-us/regions/latin-america/gsma_resources/la-economia-movil-en-america-latina-2024/)

**Critical implication:** Peru and Mexico have **below-50% banked rates**, and Peru's credit card penetration at ~9% (not ~22% as previously estimated) means requiring a credit card for subscription payment immediately excludes **~91% of Peruvian adults** from direct payment. Brazil and Chile have substantially higher financial inclusion, making traditional payment flows more viable.

*Sources: World Bank Global Findex Database 2022; GSMA Mobile Economy LatAm 2024; eMarketer 2024*
