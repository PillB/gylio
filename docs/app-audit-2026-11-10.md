# App audit, 10 November 2026

What was measured, what was wrong and why, what changed, and what is still open. Everything in
"Fixed" is in pull request #131 with tests that were checked by breaking the code on purpose and
confirming a test fails. Everything in "Open" is not done.

## How the app was measured

Three things were measured before anything was changed, so each fix has a before and an after.

1. **Every page at two sizes.** A script opened each route on a 390x844 phone and a 1280x800
   desktop, saved a full-page screenshot and counted headings, buttons, inputs and page height.
2. **Every guided tour at three sizes.** A script walked all 8 guides (47 steps) at 320x568,
   390x844 and 1280x800 and recorded, for each step, whether the target was found, whether it was
   on screen, and whether the card covered it.
3. **The code.** Import graph (which files nothing imports), bundle contents by package, complexity
   caps, the Worker's routes and their checks, and every place a transition or scroll is animated.

## Things checked and found sound

- The Worker scopes every query to the signed-in person, uses bound parameters everywhere, checks
  the token's authorised party, and answers only the allowed origins.
- The Express routes that still exist sit behind `requireAuth` and rate limits.
- No `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, no `any`, no `@ts-ignore`, no `TODO` in `src`.
- Both languages have the same keys (the build fails otherwise).

## What research says about apps built this way

- A study of 200 deployed AI-built apps (arXiv 2606.23130) found at least one vulnerability in 90%
  of them: broken access control in 36%, secrets or weak cryptography in 21%, injection in 18%. The
  causes it names are security rules that were never written down, design aimed at a demo, a control
  loosened to make something work, and a change made in one place but not the others.
- The last cause is the one this audit kept finding, in product behaviour instead of security: the
  motion setting honoured in 3 places out of 30, a tour target present in one view out of three, an
  empty state written for a layout that later changed. The fixes above each replace many copies
  with one (one attribute, one toggle component, one heading component), with a test that fails if
  a copy comes back.
- A second pattern is specific to this stack: a limit written in the wrong unit. The sync
  service's body limit counted characters, not bytes (row 15). Cloudflare D1 has its own limits
  (100 bound parameters per query; 50 queries per request on the free plan); the Worker's writes
  stay under both today, and a much larger sync payload is the case to watch.
- Sources: arXiv 2606.23130; Invicti's report on secrets in AI-generated code; Cloudflare's D1
  limits page.

## Trade-offs made

- **The graph and animation libraries were removed, not loaded on demand.** A routine's steps are a
  straight sequence; a plain list shows that. If branching routines are ever built, a graph comes
  back then.
- **A guide no longer pulls you back to its tab.** Open another tab mid-step and you stay there; the
  card stays on screen with Next and Back. The old behaviour is what caused the address loop.
- **The Social guide is hidden on plans without Social.** It walked through a locked screen.
- **Daily view is a switch among the preferences**, not a section of its own with a button.
- **Empty sections are hidden, not explained.** Someone new to Calendar sees the grid and "Add
  event" and nothing about focus suggestions until they have a task to suggest. The guide and the
  page introduction still describe them.
- **Four help buttons on Budget were removed** (period, and a transaction's amount, date and notes).
  Their text repeated the label or the heading's own help.
- **Opening Pricing, Privacy or Terms now takes one more request** the first time (30 kB for the
  legal page). The installed app caches all of them, so they still open offline.
- **No analytics events were added** in the Settings and empty-state work: it removes controls and
  text and adds no new action to count. The route-problem page and the guide changes do report.
